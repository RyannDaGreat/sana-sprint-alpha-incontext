H3 Magic: a sampling decoder for H3's latent

Manifest §26 (m01001). The question: does a decoder that SAMPLES (diffusion or flow, conditioned on H3's latent) get more out of the same latent than H3's own regression decoder? All numbers are measured on the same 68 held-out images and the same latents, against the source pixels.

Verdict. No answer yet: round 1 is training. The baseline numbers below are measured.

  • H3's own decoder on 68 held-out 512² images: PSNR 30.42 dB, DISTS 0.070, LPIPS-vgg 0.159, 714 ms per image (A100)
  • §25 optimized latent through H3's decoder (12 images): PSNR 35.22 dB, DISTS 0.024: the regression upper bound
  • FLUX.2 VAE round trip of the source: PSNR 38.89 dB, DISTS 0.012: the ceiling of a decoder that ends in the FLUX.2 decoder

Round 1 of 3 running (training on node 2 since 5:07 AM ET; first scores about 6:30 AM ET)

Headline numbers, in plain words

No sampling method has passed both gates yet (none is scored, or none keeps the information): only the references are shown, as the scale of what is on the table.

  • ΔPSNR (single sample) = mean over images of [PSNR(method, seed 0) − PSNR(H3 decoder)]. Higher is better. A perfect posterior sampler loses 3.01 dB here.
  • ΔPSNR (mean of 8) = mean over images of [PSNR(mean of 8 seeds' decodes) − PSNR(H3 decoder)]. This estimates what the decoder keeps of the latent's information.
  • ΔDISTS, ΔLPIPS-vgg = mean over images of [score(method) − score(H3 decoder)]. Lower is better, so negative means the method wins. In brackets: 95% bootstrap CI of that mean over images (paired, 2000 resamples).
  • Share of the §25 gap closed (PSNR) = (PSNRmethod − PSNRH3) / (PSNRoptimized − PSNRH3), on the images that all three decoded (the 12 with §25's optimized latent). DISTS: (DISTSH3 − DISTSmethod) / (DISTSH3 − DISTSoptimized). 100% = as good as the best latent that regression decoding can reach; negative = worse than H3.

Method: the fitness rule, the test set, the data, the compute

Test set: 68 held-out 512² images (8 magick, 8 pexbg, 32 pexels_spare, 20 pexels_val); 12 have §25's optimized latent. Data: ~80,000 training images (Pexels clips frames 0 to 6 s and MAGICK images, train splits only) with their H3 posterior-sample latents; no test image or test clip was trained on. Everything is in Bluejay (dd_samples, dd_methods, dd_renders; Sparrow views view_dd_*).

The fitness rule (FITNESS.md, written before round 1)

Test set (CONTRACT.md): 68 held-out 512² images, never trained on

20 pexels_val (frame 70 of the 20 held-out clips of the decoder frenzy / §25), 32 pexels_spare (frame at 3 s of 32 spare clips, never in any training set), 8 magick (Latent Compositor test foregrounds on 50% gray), 8 pexbg (their Pexels backgrounds; the 6 of their clips that were in H3's train split were removed from DiffDec's training set). 12 of them have §25's optimized latent. Every decoder decodes the SAME latent per image: H3's encoder posterior SAMPLE (seed 20261122, untiled encode), the latent the pipeline trains on and generates. Mode latents are a secondary eval.

Measured for every candidate and reference (dd_eval.py -> results.json -> BlueJay dd_methods / dd_renders)

Against the SOURCE pixels, with the §25 judges (h3_enc_lib.Judges, so numbers compare with h3_enc_results): PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS-alex, LPIPS-vgg, DISTS, ΔE2000, FLUX.2 latent MSE, sharp_grad and sharp_fft (decode / source; judged as |1 − ratio|: above 1 = grain or ringing, below 1 = blur), psnr_mean8 and dists_mean8 (the mean of 8 seeds' decodes: an estimate of the posterior mean), seed_std (pixel std across seeds), ms per 512² image (batch 1), params, training GPU-hours.

References (g0/): h3_decoder (THE baseline), h3_decoder_mode, optimized_l1vgg (§25 upper bound of regression decoding, 12 images), flux2_oracle (FLUX.2 VAE round trip of the source: the ceiling of any decoder that ends in the FLUX.2 decoder).

Fitness (selection between rounds)

  • Gate 1 (information is kept): psnr_mean8 >= PSNR(h3_decoder) − 1.0 dB.
  • Gate 2 (a single sample is usable): PSNR >= PSNR(h3_decoder) − 3.0 dB (the −3.01 dB of a perfect posterior sampler).
  • Rank the gate passers by DISTS (lower is better; no child may train on DISTS, so it is a held-out judge); ties (within 0.002) by LPIPS-vgg, then |1 − sharp_fft|.
  • Speed is reported, never traded against quality in round 1 (the user: "maybe a faster model").
  • Fair comparison: same 68 images, same latents, the judges above; training budget per run = 2 H100 x <= 80 min (2.7 H100-hours; children trained on A100s get the same GPU-hour count x 2); timings re-measured on one A100 alone.

The headline question: does a sampling decoder get MORE out of H3's latent than H3's decoder does?

YES only if a sampling candidate, on the 68 images: 1. beats h3_decoder on DISTS and LPIPS-vgg, with a paired bootstrap 95% CI (over images) that excludes 0, AND 2. keeps Gate 1, AND 3. wins a blind A/B by eye vs h3_decoder (fresh Sonnet agents, no context, 1:1 crops, sides random; > 50% with a binomial 95% CI above 50%), AND 4. beats its own deterministic twin (the isolating ablation), so the gain comes from sampling and not only from the loss. Strong YES: psnr_mean8 > PSNR(h3_decoder) (the regression decoder is not even the best mean estimate: information left on the table). For the refiner: (refiner − refiner_nolat) on DISTS / psnr_mean8 = the information the decoder took from the latent beyond H3's decode. Images first; 141-frame video only if images win.

No sampling decoder for the H3 latent (or any 16x video latent with a frozen encoder) exists. So the first baseline is the closest published recipe adapted to H3: SSDD's (flow matching + 0.5 LPIPS on x0-hat, upsample + concat, U-ViT), not a download. SSDD's weights decode other latents (4-64 ch), so they cannot be reused; its code is the reference.

Glossary
H3 latent
MiniMax H3's compressed picture: 24 numbers per 16×16 pixel cell (24×32×32 for a 512² image), normalized per channel. Here always the encoder's posterior sample (seed 20261122), the latent the pipeline trains on and generates.
H3 decoder (g0/h3_decoder)
H3's own decoder, a regression decoder trained to minimize a pixel-style error, run in bf16 on 256-px tiles. The baseline every candidate must beat.
Sampling decoder
A decoder that starts from random noise and removes it step by step (diffusion or flow matching), conditioned on the latent. The same latent can give many different, equally valid images.
Posterior sample
One random draw from all the images that fit a latent. A perfect sampler's single draw has twice the squared error of the average image, i.e. 3.01 dB lower PSNR (Blau & Michaeli 2018).
Mean-of-8
The average of the decodes of 8 different noise seeds: an estimate of the posterior mean. Its PSNR says how much information the decoder kept; it is what Gate 1 checks.
Flow matching
A way to train a noise-to-image generator: the net predicts the straight-line velocity from noise to image, and sampling integrates it in a few steps.
Deterministic twin
The same network and losses as a sampling candidate but without noise (a regression decoder). It separates "sampling helps" from "the loss helps": a candidate must beat its own twin.
Refiner / PMRF
A sampling model that starts from H3's decode (not from pure noise) and corrects it (PMRF, Ohayon et al., 2024). The no-latent twin (refiner_nolat) gets no latent input; the gap between the two is the information the refiner takes from the latent beyond H3's decode.
FIBO / FLUX.2 latent space
The latent of the FLUX.2 VAE (FIBO2 normalization), 32 channels at 8× downsampling, or 128 channels on H3's 16× grid after 2×2 patchifying. A child can diffuse there and finish with the FLUX.2 decoder. g0/flux2_oracle is the round trip of the source through that VAE: the ceiling of any such decoder.
Optimized latent (g0/optimized_l1vgg)
§25: a latent found by gradient descent through H3's frozen decoder to match the source (L1 + LPIPS-vgg). The upper bound of what regression decoding can get; only 12 images have it.
PSNR
Peak signal-to-noise ratio in dB: pixel error on a log scale. Higher is better. Rewards the blurry average.
DISTS
Deep Image Structure and Texture Similarity (Ding et al. 2020): a learned perceptual distance. Lower is better. No child trains on it, so it is the held-out judge and the rank.
LPIPS (alex, vgg)
Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (Zhang et al. 2018) on AlexNet or VGG features. Lower is better. LPIPS-vgg is the second judge (children may train on LPIPS-vgg, so it is a weaker judge).
sharp HF
High-frequency FFT energy of the decode divided by the source's. Below 1 = blur; above 1 = grain or ringing.
Gate
A pass/fail check before a method may be ranked: Gate 1 keeps the information (mean-of-8), Gate 2 keeps a single sample usable.
Genetic frenzy (mini)
Several agents each propose, build and score decoders in rounds; the best are kept as parents. Here: 3 rounds × 5 children, each with a prediction written before running and an isolating ablation.
