Is H3's encoder the bottleneck?

Manifest §25 (m00992, m00993, m00994). Version 3 (6:45 AM ET): adds the tiled-encode finding (latent statistics + stock H3's loss) and two more optimized clips (5 in all). A second fine-tuned encoder, a temporal objective for fast clips, more objectives and a longer 'ceiling' optimization are running. Every image is the original lossless PNG at full resolution; zooms are cut from those files in the browser. All results live in Bluejay (h3_enc_samples, h3_enc_runs, h3_enc_results; Sparrow view view_h3_encoder).

Finding 1: H3 expects tiled-encoded latents.

The project encodes untiled (3× faster). That gives latents whose spread in channels 16–23 is only about 0.7 of what H3's own normalization expects, in the 300 stored training latents as in fresh re-encodes. The spread depends on the size of the picture the encoder sees: 128-px tiles give 1.39, the VAE's default 256-px tiles 0.96, 384-px tiles 0.76, the whole 512² frame 0.67. Only the default tiles match H3. Stock H3 agrees: its flow-matching loss on the same 30 clips is lower for tiled latents at every noise level, on 29–30 of 30 clips, about 10% lower on average. So H3 was trained on latents like the tiled ones, and every adapter of this project so far was trained on latents slightly off H3's distribution. The tiled encode also decodes better (on 18 held-out clips LPIPS 0.055 → 0.046, DISTS 0.056 → 0.047).

Finding 2 (the question asked): is the encoder the bottleneck? Yes for the metrics, partly for the eye. Latents exist that the frozen decoder turns into much better reconstructions on every reference metric (+4.1 dB PSNR on images, +2.5 dB on 5 whole clips, DISTS down by 60%), and they stay as close to the encoder's latent as the pipeline's own sampling noise does, with unchanged per-channel statistics, so H3's diffusion model should accept them. By eye the gain is truer color, skin tone and contrast and cleaner edges, not new fine detail: hair, frets and rooftops stay about as soft, so the decoder limits fine texture. A fine-tuned encoder (decoder frozen, one hour of training) captures part of it: +2.3 dB on images and +0.8 dB on clips, better on all six metrics for 48 of 52 images and 17 of 18 clips. Pixel-perfect video is not in reach with this decoder; a better encoder is a real but moderate win.

  • 12 held-out images (8 Pexels frames, 4 MAGICK on gray, 512²), latent optimized with L1 + 0.1·LPIPS-vgg for 100 Adam steps: PSNR 31.12 → 35.22 dB (every image gains, +2.0 to +6.1 dB); held-out judges (no objective used them): DISTS 0.061 → 0.024, FLUX.2 latent MSE 0.329 → 0.205, LPIPS-alex 0.055 → 0.040; all 12 better on all six of PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS-alex, DISTS, FLUX.2 MSE, CIEDE2000.
  • 5 whole 141-frame clips, 60 Adam steps through all 8 decoder chunks: PSNR 31.02 → 33.50 dB (+1.6 to +3.0 each), LPIPS-alex 0.068 → 0.048, DISTS 0.056 → 0.026; all 5 better on all six metrics. Flicker: unchanged or lower on the 3 calm clips (0.142 → 0.142, 0.58 → 0.50, 0.31 → 0.28), higher on the 2 fast ones (park 1.90 → 2.45, cliffs 1.48 → 2.28): the per-frame objective does not watch motion; a run with a temporal term is running.
  • Pure PSNR is the wrong objective: plain L2 reaches +5.1 dB but makes DISTS worse than the encoder (0.068 vs 0.061): blur. L1 + LPIPS improves every metric.
  • The optimized latents move by RMS 0.077 (images) and 0.063 (clips) from the posterior mode; the pipeline's own posterior sample moves by 0.060 / 0.039. Per-channel means shift by 0.017 on average and per-channel spreads stay within 0.985–1.043 of the encoder's.
  • Fine-tuned encoder ft1 (H3's encoder retrained 1,000 steps with the decoder frozen). Tiled or untiled? It was trained on 256² crops, so during training every input was exactly one 256-px tile (the tiled regime), and its latent penalty pulled toward H3's encoder on those same crops. It is scored both ways on the full 512² frames: tiled encode (run encft_ft1_tiled, the regime it was trained in): images 32.53 → 34.86 dB, DISTS 0.062 → 0.049; clips 33.91 → 34.73 dB, LPIPS 0.055 → 0.044, DISTS 0.056 → 0.044, flicker 0.43 → 0.45. Untiled (run encft_ft1) it gains only +1.9 dB / +0.3 dB. Part of ft1's tiled gain is the tiling itself (H3's own encoder, tiled: +0.45 dB on clips); against that tiled baseline ft1 adds about +0.4 dB on clips and +2.5 dB on images. The second fine-tune (ft2) uses the same 256² crops.
  • A free win: a tiled encode with H3's own encoder already improves clips (LPIPS 0.055 → 0.046, DISTS 0.056 → 0.047, flicker 0.43 → 0.42, +0.45 dB); the project encodes untiled for speed.
  • Blind A/B: a fresh Sonnet agent shown 24 zoomed crops (source | A | B, order random) preferred the optimized latent in 15 of 24, mostly at its lowest confidence; my own look agrees (truer skin and object colors, no chroma halos on graphics; hair, frets and rooftops about as soft).

The question (m00992): does a latent exist that the frozen H3 decoder turns into a clearly better picture than the encoder's own latent? We find one by gradient descent on the latent itself, against the source pixels, with several objectives, and judge the result with metrics the optimizer did not see and by eye on zoomed details. If such latents are much better, the encoder (not the decoder) limits H3's reconstructions, and a better encoder could be trained with the decoder frozen.

Method
  1. Samples (held out, never trained on): the 20 held-out Pexels clips of the decoder frenzy (the clips BASELINE.json scores), as whole 141-frame clips and as their frame 70 (512²), plus 8 MAGICK foregrounds over 50% gray and their Pexels backgrounds at 512² and 768².
  2. Encoder baselines: the same untiled encode as the project. Posterior sample is the latent the pipeline trains on (seeded); mode drops that noise; tiled encodes in overlapping 256² tiles.
  3. Latent optimization: Adam on the whole latent, starting at the posterior mode, learning rate 0.01 with a cosine decay to 0.001, 100 steps for images (60 for clips). The decoder is frozen and runs exactly as the pipeline decodes: bf16, 256-pixel tiles, one image decoded as its latent repeated 7 times (frame 0 kept), a clip's 8 temporal chunks together. Gradient checkpointing per decoder block; for clips the loss is taken frame batch by frame batch on a detached copy of the decode, then one backward through the decoder. Cost on one A100: about 2 s per image per step, 22 s per clip per step.
  4. Objectives: l2, l1 (pixels), lpips_vgg, lpips_alex, flux (MSE between FLUX.2-VAE latents of the decode and of the source; FIBO2's image encoder), l1_vgg = L1 + 0.1·LPIPS-vgg, l1_vgg_flux = l1_vgg + 0.01·flux, and l1_vgg with a prior that keeps the latent inside the encoder's spread.
  5. Judges: PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS (alex and vgg), DISTS (no objective uses it), FLUX.2 latent MSE, CIEDE2000, sharpness ratios, flicker (clips), all vs the source; then zoomed crops viewed by eye and a blind A/B by a fresh model agent.
  6. Fine-tuned encoder: a copy of H3's encoder trained with the decoder frozen on Bluejay train data (train Pexels clips and MAGICK images, never a held-out clip), 256² crops (one decoder tile), 34-frame windows (two encoder chunks, decoded as one 22-frame decoder chunk) plus single frames, loss L1 + 0.1·LPIPS-vgg plus a penalty on (z − zH3)² to stay in the latent distribution H3's diffusion model knows; then the same held-out scoring.
Glossary
Latent
H3's compressed picture: 24 numbers per 16×16 pixel cell (and per 4 frames in video). One 512² image = 24×1×32×32; a 141-frame clip = 24×42×32×32. Normalized: per channel, (z − mean) / std, so each number has about unit spread over the data.
Encoder latent (posterior sample / mode)
The encoder outputs a mean and a spread per number. The project's pipeline draws a random sample from it (seeded); the mode is the mean itself, without that noise.
Tiled encode
Encoding in overlapping 256×256 tiles. The project encodes untiled (3× faster); the decoder always decodes in tiles.
Optimized latent
A latent found by gradient descent (Adam) through the frozen decoder, starting at the posterior mode, so that its decode matches the source under one objective.
Objective
What the optimizer minimizes: l2 / l1 (pixel error), lpips_vgg / lpips_alex (a learned perceptual distance), flux (the distance between FLUX.2-VAE latents of decode and source: FIBO2's image encoder), and mixes, e.g. l1_vgg = L1 + 0.1·LPIPS-vgg.
Prior
An extra penalty that keeps the latent inside the encoder's spread: mean(((z − mode) / spread)²).
Fine-tuned encoder
A copy of H3's encoder trained with the decoder frozen to make decodes match their sources, plus a penalty on how far its latents move from the original encoder's.
DISTS
A perceptual image distance (structure and texture similarity, Ding et al. 2020) that no objective here optimizes: a held-out judge.
Sharpness (grad / HF)
Mean Sobel gradient and high-frequency FFT energy of the decode divided by the source's: below 1 = blurrier than the source.
Flicker
|mean frame-to-frame change of the decode − that of the source| on 0–255: extra or missing temporal change.
