H3 Magic: split v3-grid (gray, white / black, alpha in one 2×2 frame)

Part of Alpha Video v2. Every item is the unmodified output; the |Δα| heat maps are drawn from the two alphas.

The idea (m01003, m01005, m01006). Instead of three output heads, H3 draws one 768×768 frame made of four 384×384 panels: top left the subject on 50% gray, top right on white, bottom left on black, bottom right its alpha matte in grayscale (white = opaque). Because the four panels are one image, attention is shared and the subject should match across them. The three background panels give the colour (decontamination: solve for the foreground colour given the known backgrounds), and the 4th panel is the alpha (the mask). The alpha that the three backgrounds imply on their own (least squares) is a free check: how much the two disagree, |Δα|, is reported per image.

Training. One LoRA on H3 + gray magic v3t (gray magic v3 fine-tuned on tiled latents), trained on the 10,000 MAGICK images laid out as such grids (tiled VAE encode, manifest §25), guidance loss (scale 4), lr 1e-4. Caption = the subject-only caption + this sentence (also at the end of every test prompt, q01–q20): Shown as a 2x2 grid of the same image: top left on a plain flat medium-gray (#808080) background, top right on a plain white background, bottom left on a plain black background, bottom right its grayscale alpha matte, white subject on black.

First look (8:25 AM, training step 50; still training)

The layout works after only 50 training steps (row av2_grid_s00050_image_fgrid, the 20 subject-only prompts with the grid sentence): H3 draws a clean 2×2 frame with the same subject, pose and colours in all four panels.

But the 4th-panel alpha is a coarse, over-smoothed silhouette that leaves out real parts of the subject. The chef (q04): the matte misses the wok (the whole pan is black in the matte), the flying vegetables and the flame, the plate is a rough blob and the edges are cartoon-smooth. The coffee (q17): the matte misses the pouring stream and the steam, and the pot is clipped. Decontaminating with this mask cuts those parts off. Where the subject is one solid body (the skater, the cat, the cyclist), the matte is clean and fully solid, and white on white is solved: the white cup and saucer are opaque, unlike with the three-head split.

The core number is near 1 partly because the matte is a blob: it measures holes inside the silhouette, not missing parts. Missed subject (share of pixels that the three backgrounds call solid, alpha ≥ 0.9, but the 4th panel calls clear, < 0.5): mean 0.76% of the frame, worst the wave (q19, 10.7%, IoU 0.66) and the spinning top (q16, 2.0%, IoU 0.60); the reverse (panel solid, backgrounds clear) 0.40%; IoU of the two masks 0.89. The chef's wok scores only 0.7% because on the black panel the black wok also fools the three-background estimate.

Disagreement |Δ&alpha| between the 4th-panel alpha and the three-background alpha: all pixels mean 0.038, median 0.006, 95th percentile 0.208; edge band (0.02 < α < 0.98) mean 0.231, median 0.151, 95th percentile 0.689. Most of the edge disagreement is the over-smoothed matte (thin and semi-transparent parts the panel leaves out or blurs), plus thin dark contour lines of the three-background estimate along internal edges.

Open question: whether thin structures (stream, steam, flying pieces) come with more training steps, or whether 384-pixel panels (one latent patch = 32 pixels of the panel) are too coarse for them. Checked at steps 100, 150 and 200 below; grid videos follow.
