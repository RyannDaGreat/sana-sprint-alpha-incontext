H3 Magic: a fast decoder for H3, by genetic search

The MiniMax H3 video VAE decoder is a 36-layer transformer. It is slow. We want a small network that turns an H3 latent into video frames much faster, close enough to the full decode to use for previews. We search for it with a genetic frenzy: rounds of Sonnet agents that mutate and cross the best candidates, run opportunistically on whatever GPU is idle. There are three speed prizes, 10×, 30× and 100× faster than the full decode, each won by the best quality inside it, and a fourth, TQ, for a decoder that is better than the original at up to twice its time.

Status (round 0, no candidate trained yet). This page shows what exists now: why, how the FLUX.2 predecessor was made, the H3 facts, the starting design, the rules, and the target quality. Results appear here after every round. Last updated: round 0.

Why a fast decoder

H3 Magic generates video from latents. To see anything we must decode, and the full decoder takes seconds for a clip. A fast decoder lets us show previews while a generation runs (the same idea as the FLUX.2 sneak peek) and makes evaluation and data inspection cheap. Previews do not need to be perfect, but they must look like the real decode: same colors, same motion, no flicker.

Facts about the H3 decoder

  • 36-layer ViT decoder, width 2048, one token per latent voxel (patch 4×16×16: 4 frames and 16×16 pixels per token), run in chunks of 7 latent frames with 256×256 spatial tiles.
  • Latent for a 141-frame, 512×512 clip: (24, 42, 32, 32) = (channels, latent frames, height, width). The encoder reads 17-frame clips = 5 latent frames; the first covers 1 pixel frame, the others 4 each (141 frames = 42 latent frames).
  • Full decode time of one such latent, measured on an A100 (H3Engine bench): 42.9 s in fp32, 6.48 s in bf16 (median of the last 3 of 4 runs; bf16 matches fp32 to 53 dB PSNR). The reference for the speedup is the bf16 time.

How the FLUX.2 fast decoder was made (the predecessor)

For the FLUX.2 VAE (49.6M-parameter decoder, 207 ms per 1024×1024 image) an earlier genetic search produced r5a3_long, now shipped: 0.88M parameters, 6.5 ms, 32× faster, SSIM 0.980 against the original images (the full decoder itself scores 0.985). It took 5 rounds of 5 Sonnet agents (188 candidates in all) and about 2.5 GPU hours of training.

What it is

  1. Patchify the latent 2×2 and map it back to the latent grid with the fal Tiny AE's transposed convolution.
  2. A TAESD-style decoder cut at ¼ resolution: residual conv blocks at ⅛ and ¼ pixel resolution only. The ½-resolution and full-resolution stages, where most pixels and time are, are dropped.
  3. A head: conv to 128 channels, PixelShuffle(4) to 8 channels at full resolution, then three cheap parallel 3×3 branches summed (8→3, 8→16→3, 8→48→3; the last two start at zero).
  4. Trained by distillation from the full decoder: L1 + (1−SSIM) + 0.15·LPIPS against the teacher output, plus 0.5·L1 against the source image and 0.5·L1 in Lab color.

Why it won

  • The big idea was a round-2 wildcard: decode at low resolution and upsample with a learned sub-pixel layer. Cutting at ⅛ resolution fails (dotted grid, SSIM 0.77); at ¼ it works.
  • It started from weights that already imitated the FLUX.2 decoder. Random-init students were worse.
  • It removed the expensive stages instead of narrowing every stage; narrowing blurred faces.
  • Output distillation plus a Lab color loss beat feature hints 5 of 5 times; Lab L1 fixed oversaturation (ΔE 2.71 → 1.08).
  • Later rounds were safe mutations: each new branch was zero-initialized, so a child started exactly at its parent and could only improve.

Round 0: the starting design for H3

6.66M parameters. A new 3D convolution front on the latent grid (Conv3d 24→192 and 3 residual blocks with 3×3×3 temporal mixing, 5.88M) emits 4 frames × 64 channels at ⅛ resolution per latent frame. Those feed the r5a3_long tail unchanged, with its weights loaded (0.78M). For a clip-start latent frame only the 4th output is kept (the VAE drops 3 padding frames), so one image latent gives 1 frame. Losses are r5a3_long's, with the full H3 bf16 decode (with tiling, as the pipeline decodes) as teacher. Data: 47 training and 20 held-out Pexels clips (141 frames, 24 fps, 512² center crop). Open question: whether the H3 latent space maps well onto a tail trained on FLUX.2.

Speed so far: 145 ms for all 141 frames (fp16 autocast, untrained weights, shared GPU, not a clean timing), against 6.48 s for the bf16 full decode: about 45×. Quality is not measured yet; no GPU has been free.

The four prizes and the fitness rule

speedup = (full H3 VAE decode, bf16, of one (24, 42, 32, 32) latent → 141 frames at 512²) / (candidate time), both measured on the same GPU in the same window, CUDA-synchronized, median of at least 5 runs after warmup. Reference: 6.48 s, so the time limits are below (to be re-measured in a clean window).

T10 prize: speedup ≥ 10× (about ≤ 648 ms for 141 frames)
T30 prize: speedup ≥ 30× (about ≤ 216 ms)
T100 prize: speedup ≥ 100× (about ≤ 65 ms)
TQ prize, pure quality (no speed goal): time at most 2× the full decode (bf16, same GPU, same window). Eligible only if every score beats the original H3 decoder, both measured against the source frames: PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, ΔE2000 color error and flicker. Among eligible candidates the highest PSNR against the source wins. The question: can we make a better decoder than the original? Starting points: the full H3 decoder fine-tuned on (latent → source) pairs, or the full decoder plus a learned residual refiner.

Inside a tier the best candidate is ranked by SSIM against the full decode on the 20 held-out clips, then LPIPS (alex), then flicker (mean |Δframe| difference against the full decode's). A look at sample frames vetoes artifacts the numbers miss. Winners get final clean timings when GPUs are free. Full details: agents/.scratchpad/fastdec_ga/FITNESS.md.

Method. 5 rounds of 5 Sonnet agents; each child is a mutation of a tier leader, a crossover of two leaders, or a wildcard (at least one wildcard per round, at most 2 children per lineage). Groups of 2–3 meet in a mailbox channel to breed. Every candidate is kept with a genome card. This search has the lowest GPU priority: it only uses a GPU nobody else is using, checkpoints every few minutes, and does CPU-side design work while no GPU is free.

Glossary

Genetic frenzy
Successive rounds of parallel agents that evolve candidates for one measurable problem: select the fittest, breed children by mutation (change one thing in a leader), crossover (combine two leaders) or wildcard (a new idea), keep every candidate with a genome card.
Genome card
A JSON record per candidate: what it is, parents, the change, scores, verdict.
Tier / prize
A speed class (10×, 30×, 100× faster than the full decode) or the TQ quality class; each has its own leader and winner.
TQ
The pure quality prize: at most 2× the full decode's time, must beat the original on every score against the source frames.
Full decode / teacher
The H3 VAE's own 36-layer decoder in bf16. The fast decoder is trained to imitate it and is scored against it.
Latent (24, 42, 32, 32)
The compressed video: 24 channels, 42 latent frames (each 4 pixel frames, the first of each 17-frame clip 1), 32×32 spatial (each 16×16 pixels).
SSIM
Structural similarity, 1 = identical.
LPIPS (alex)
Learned perceptual distance, 0 = identical.
Flicker
Mean absolute difference between neighboring frames; we compare the candidate's with the full decode's to see if it adds or hides motion noise.
Held-out clips
20 Pexels clips never used for training; all scores use them.
PixelShuffle
A layer that turns channels into pixels (4× upsample from 16 channels per pixel), cheap and learned.
bf16 / fp16 / fp32
Number formats; lower precision is faster on a GPU.
Distillation
Training a small network to reproduce a big one's output.
Zero-init branch
A new layer started at zero, so a child begins exactly equal to its parent.
Low priority GPU use
This search takes only idle GPUs; higher-priority jobs may kill its processes at any time.
