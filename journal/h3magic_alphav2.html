H3 Magic: Alpha Video v2 (gray by prompt, 768×768, two adapters)

Every image and video is the unmodified output (768×768). Times are Eastern.

Goal (m00983). A better cut-out (RGBA) video model than Alpha Video 1 (the split adapter of the split page), by 3 PM ET on 2026-11-23.

The trick. First ask stock H3, with words alone, to put the subject on a flat gray background. If a prompt suffix does that, every caption and every test prompt of this version carries the suffix, so the gray magic adapter has little left to learn: mostly MAGICK's over-saturated look. Then only two adapters, both trained on still images at 768×768 (512 was low): the gray magic adapter, and the split adapter on top of it (gray, white and black outputs, least-squares RGBA). No Pexels and no image-gen adapter: the "basic ass magic" ablation showed they are not needed. Training data: the dataset page.

Entry 1: prompt-only gray (stock H3, no adapter)

Stock H3 at CFG 1, the 20 subject-only prompts with their fixed seeds, 768×768 images, once per candidate suffix (round 1: 12 phrasings; round 2: 4 variants of the round-1 winner). Score: share of border pixels that are a flat neutral mid-gray (see the glossary); also the spread (std) and mean level of the border, 50% gray = 128.

Finding (2:25 AM). Words alone get most of the way. With no suffix, stock H3 invents scenery for 19 of 20 subjects (6% of border pixels are neutral gray). The best phrasing, hex_medium: "The subject is isolated on a plain flat medium-gray (#808080) background, like a cut-out on an empty gray canvas.", puts every subject on a flat gray: 92% of border pixels neutral mid-gray, border level 144 (128 is exact 50% gray), the smallest spread of all phrasings. Naming the hex color and "cut-out" is what made it flat (no floor, no shadows); "medium" brought the level down from 158 to 144; "50% gray" made it worse (173, lighter). Costs: some subjects come out small (the skater), and the wave becomes a cut-out slab. Test videos with the suffix keep their motion (the dancer spins, the cat pounces) on a gray studio, with a lighter floor and soft shadows. Chosen: hex_medium. Every caption and test prompt of Alpha Video v2 carries it.

Entry 2: gray magic v3 (training, tasting)

Run. One LoRA (rank 16) on stock H3, trained on the 10,000 MAGICK images on 50% gray at 768×768 with their gray-suffix captions (dataset page). Guidance loss (scale 4, as in §22: keeps H3's distilled guidance, so the adapter works at CFG 1), learning rate 1e-4, 64 images per step on node 2's 8 GPUs, about 7 s per step. Started 2:28 AM.

Validation loss (fixed 20 held-out images, 3 noise levels): step 1: 6.39, step 50: 3.66, step 100: 3.17.

Tasting at step 100 (2:47 AM; 6 subject-only prompts + the suffix, CFG 1, same seeds; top: stock H3 with the suffix, bottom: + gray magic v3 step 100). The gray goes from a light, uneven gray (border level 144) to a flat gray: border level 122 (0.48; 50% gray is 128), spread 9.9 (stock 18.9), 97% of border pixels within ±16 of 128 (stock 39%). Subjects stay intact and come out a little larger; no MAGICK over-saturation yet.

Entry 3: split v3 (training, tasting)

Run. The split adapter of §22 (a LoRA + three output heads, gray / white / black; least squares gives the cut-out) trained at 768×768 on gray magic v3 (baked in), on the same 10,000 MAGICK images on gray, white and black with the gray-suffix captions. Same recipe as Alpha Video 1's winner: the gray head keeps the guidance loss (scale 4), white and black learn the gray target plus their exact difference from gray, the head's Δ at 10× the LoRA's learning rate (1e-4). Node 2, 8 GPUs, 64 images per step, about 7 s per step. Started 3:02 AM.

Validation loss per head (gray / white / black): step 1: 2.94 / 3.47 / 3.48; step 50: 2.95 / 3.07 / 2.85; step 100: 2.98 / 3.05 / 2.84; step 150: 3.00 / 3.06 / 2.86. The gray head stays near its start (+2%), the white and black heads drop in the first 50 steps and then stay flat.

Tasting (rows av2_split_sNNNNN_image_fgray below: gray magic v3 + the split at that step, the 20 subject-only prompts + suffix). The validation loss is flat after step 50, but the cut-out keeps improving: the mean alpha in the image border (the background, which should be 0) over 6 prompts is 0.30 at step 50, 0.10 at step 100 and 0.07 at step 150 (white head: from light gray to near-pure white; black head: from dark gray to near-black). So the 3:23 AM automatic stop at step 168 (it watched the flat validation loss) was early; training was resumed at 3:28 AM and runs to 4:00 AM. Still visible at step 150: a soft halo about one patch wide around each subject, and white parts of white objects (the cup) partly transparent, as in Alpha Video 1.

Entry 4: first test results (gray magic v3 + split v3 vs Alpha Video 1)

The mean alpha in the image border (background, ideally 0) is 0.118 vs 0.212 for Alpha Video 1, and the least-squares fit residual 0.018 vs 0.025 (lower = the three heads agree better). The border number includes subjects that touch the frame (the chef's wok, the wave), which are correctly opaque. Still there: a soft halo about one patch wide, and white parts of white objects (cup, saucer) partly transparent.

Entry 5: the video readout fixes the drift (the main result)

Why the split drifted. The split adapter only ever saw single images (one latent frame). In a video, H3's VAE packs about 3.4 video frames into every latent frame after the first, so those rows look different to the split head, and the white and black outputs fall back toward gray (a gray veil, about 0.2–0.3 alpha in the background).

The fix. Keep everything that makes the video (H3 + gray magic v3 + the split LoRA) frozen, and train only the split head's small last layer (Δ, 5120 → 3×96) on video: 497 synthetic clips (table h3_motion_clips) in which a MAGICK cut-out moves along a smooth random path (zoom 0.55–0.95, shift up to 18%, rotation up to 8°) for 141 frames at 768×768, composited exactly on gray, white and black. The head learns, at the noise level of the sampler's last step, that white minus gray and black minus gray must equal the true differences. Because the trunk is frozen, the gray video is unchanged: byte-identical on 20 of 20 videos. 218 steps on node 2, about 35 minutes; validation (20 held-out clips) 0.40 / 0.30 → 0.056 / 0.055 for white / black.

What is still wrong (frame 96, full resolution: gray | alpha | RGBA over the checkerboard; crops are 384×384 pixels at 1:1):

  • Subjects are not fully solid. Inside the subject core the mean alpha is 0.937 (1 is solid), and 24% of core pixels are below 0.9. That is barely better than before the readout (0.933) and Alpha Video 1 (0.921): the readout fixed the background, not the subject.
  • White on white (coffee, g17): the white saucer and the cup's white sides come out partly see-through. On a white background a white object and the background look the same, so the split cannot tell them apart.
  • Chef (g04): blocky 16-pixel patches in the smoke and flame, where the alpha changes from one latent patch to the next, and blotchy holes on the face and the white jacket (alpha well below 1 on solid cloth).
  • Skater (g01): a faint light halo along the arms and the edges of the body, and uneven gray patches inside the matte.

Entry 6: chasing the solidity flaw (three checks, no fix yet)

Entry 5's remaining flaw is that subjects are not fully solid (subject-core alpha 0.937 in video, 0.96 in images). Three checks, 6:00–7:55 AM:

  • Is it the VAE? No. Decoding the true gray, white and black latents of the 20 held-out MAGICK images and fitting the same least squares gives core alpha 0.996 (true matte 1.000) and border alpha 0.004. So H3's VAE can carry a solid matte; the flaw is in what the model predicts.
  • Weight the subject in the video readout? No gain. Continuing the readout with subject pixels (where white and black agree) weighted 5× changed core alpha from 0.925 to 0.926 on the 4 tasting videos (border 0.016 → 0.013). The head-only readout has reached its limit on solidity. (That run also crashed at step 80 for a data-loading reason, since fixed.)
  • Tiled latents? Another agent found that H3 expects latents encoded with 256-pixel tiles, while this project encoded untiled (channels 16–23 under-spread, stock H3's loss ~10% higher; manifest §25). Since 7:00 AM every new encode in the project is tiled (h3magic/h3.py encode now defaults to tiled). A/B: 150 more steps of split v3 on tiled vs untiled latents gave core alpha 0.959 vs 0.961 and border median 0.016 vs 0.022 on the 20 images: inconclusive, because gray magic v3 underneath was itself trained on untiled latents.

Next (the user's rule m01003: solidity stopped improving): a tiled fine-tune of gray magic v3 (gray magic v3t), then the 2×2 grid split, which predicts the alpha directly as a 4th panel.

Glossary
H3
MiniMax H3, a text-to-video model (480p class). We use it at 512 × 512, 141 frames (5.875 s) at 24 fps, 30 steps. It can also make a single frame, which is an image.
Adapter / LoRA
A small set of extra weights (rank 16) trained on top of a big model. It is a weight difference Δ that gets added to the model.
Bake
Add an adapter into the model weights for good (weight 1), then train the next adapter on top. Each adapter here is trained on a model with all earlier adapters baked in.
MAGICK
Our dataset of images of objects, with alpha mattes. Here we use its RGB composited on a 50% gray background, 512 × 512.
Gray magic
The adapter trained on 10,000 MAGICK images on 50% gray. The captions never mention the gray, so the adapter must learn it. The goal: a subject on a flat gray background.
Split adapter (Δ_split)
The adapter on this page: a LoRA plus a difference Δ on H3's last layer that makes three outputs (gray, white, black background) instead of one.
Head
One of the three copies of the last layer: the gray head (the model's usual output), the white head and the black head.
γ (gamma)
How much the split adapter is applied. 0 = off (the three heads are equal and the gray one is exactly the base model), 1 = fully on.
Alpha
Transparency per pixel. Shown as gray: white = opaque subject, black = clear background.
RGBA
The cut-out: color plus alpha. Shown over a checkerboard, so clear parts show the checkerboard.
Least squares fit
Per pixel, the alpha and color that best explain the three images C_k = α·F + (1 − α)·b_k for backgrounds b_k = 0.5, 1, 0 (iterative, box-constrained to [0, 1]).
Residual
How far the three heads are from one consistent cut-out (RMS, 0 to 1). 0 means they agree perfectly.
CFG
Classifier-free guidance scale. CFG 1 = one model pass per step (H3's design). CFG 2.5 = an extra pass with an empty prompt, applied to every head.
basic ass magic
The ablation adapter: one LoRA trained on gray MAGICK images directly on stock H3, with no Pexels or image-gen stage.
Suffix
A sentence added to the end of every prompt and caption, here the one that asks for a flat gray background.
Border score
The fraction of pixels in the outer 8% band of the image that are a neutral mid-gray: no hue (max − min channel ≤ 12 of 255) and a level between 96 and 176 (50% gray is 128).
Subject-only prompts
The 20 test prompts f01–f20 that describe only the subject and its motion, never a background.
