H3 Magic: Alpha Video v2 (gray by prompt, 768×768, two adapters)

Every image and video is the unmodified output (768×768). Times are Eastern.

Goal (m00983). A better cut-out (RGBA) video model than Alpha Video 1 (the split adapter of the split page), by 3 PM ET on 2026-11-23.

The trick. First ask stock H3, with words alone, to put the subject on a flat gray background. If a prompt suffix does that, every caption and every test prompt of this version carries the suffix, so the gray magic adapter has little left to learn: mostly MAGICK's over-saturated look. Then only two adapters, both trained on still images at 768×768 (512 was low): the gray magic adapter, and the split adapter on top of it (gray, white and black outputs, least-squares RGBA). No Pexels and no image-gen adapter: the "basic ass magic" ablation showed they are not needed. Training data: the dataset page.

Entry 1: prompt-only gray (stock H3, no adapter)

Stock H3 at CFG 1, the 20 subject-only prompts with their fixed seeds, 768×768 images, once per candidate suffix (round 1: 12 phrasings; round 2: 4 variants of the round-1 winner). Score: share of border pixels that are a flat neutral mid-gray (see the glossary); also the spread (std) and mean level of the border, 50% gray = 128.

Finding (2:25 AM). Words alone get most of the way. With no suffix, stock H3 invents scenery for 19 of 20 subjects (6% of border pixels are neutral gray). The best phrasing, hex_medium: "The subject is isolated on a plain flat medium-gray (#808080) background, like a cut-out on an empty gray canvas.", puts every subject on a flat gray: 92% of border pixels neutral mid-gray, border level 144 (128 is exact 50% gray), the smallest spread of all phrasings. Naming the hex color and "cut-out" is what made it flat (no floor, no shadows); "medium" brought the level down from 158 to 144; "50% gray" made it worse (173, lighter). Costs: some subjects come out small (the skater), and the wave becomes a cut-out slab. Test videos with the suffix keep their motion (the dancer spins, the cat pounces) on a gray studio, with a lighter floor and soft shadows. Chosen: hex_medium. Every caption and test prompt of Alpha Video v2 carries it.

Entry 2: gray magic v3 (training, tasting)

Run. One LoRA (rank 16) on stock H3, trained on the 10,000 MAGICK images on 50% gray at 768×768 with their gray-suffix captions (dataset page). Guidance loss (scale 4, as in §22: keeps H3's distilled guidance, so the adapter works at CFG 1), learning rate 1e-4, 64 images per step on node 2's 8 GPUs, about 7 s per step. Started 2:28 AM.

Validation loss (fixed 20 held-out images, 3 noise levels): step 1: 6.39, step 50: 3.66, step 100: 3.17.

Tasting at step 100 (2:47 AM; 6 subject-only prompts + the suffix, CFG 1, same seeds; top: stock H3 with the suffix, bottom: + gray magic v3 step 100). The gray goes from a light, uneven gray (border level 144) to a flat gray: border level 122 (0.48; 50% gray is 128), spread 9.9 (stock 18.9), 97% of border pixels within ±16 of 128 (stock 39%). Subjects stay intact and come out a little larger; no MAGICK over-saturation yet.

Glossary
H3
MiniMax H3, a text-to-video model (480p class). We use it at 512 × 512, 141 frames (5.875 s) at 24 fps, 30 steps. It can also make a single frame, which is an image.
Adapter / LoRA
A small set of extra weights (rank 16) trained on top of a big model. It is a weight difference Δ that gets added to the model.
Bake
Add an adapter into the model weights for good (weight 1), then train the next adapter on top. Each adapter here is trained on a model with all earlier adapters baked in.
MAGICK
Our dataset of images of objects, with alpha mattes. Here we use its RGB composited on a 50% gray background, 512 × 512.
Gray magic
The adapter trained on 10,000 MAGICK images on 50% gray. The captions never mention the gray, so the adapter must learn it. The goal: a subject on a flat gray background.
Split adapter (Δ_split)
The adapter on this page: a LoRA plus a difference Δ on H3's last layer that makes three outputs (gray, white, black background) instead of one.
Head
One of the three copies of the last layer: the gray head (the model's usual output), the white head and the black head.
γ (gamma)
How much the split adapter is applied. 0 = off (the three heads are equal and the gray one is exactly the base model), 1 = fully on.
Alpha
Transparency per pixel. Shown as gray: white = opaque subject, black = clear background.
RGBA
The cut-out: color plus alpha. Shown over a checkerboard, so clear parts show the checkerboard.
Least squares fit
Per pixel, the alpha and color that best explain the three images C_k = α·F + (1 − α)·b_k for backgrounds b_k = 0.5, 1, 0 (iterative, box-constrained to [0, 1]).
Residual
How far the three heads are from one consistent cut-out (RMS, 0 to 1). 0 means they agree perfectly.
CFG
Classifier-free guidance scale. CFG 1 = one model pass per step (H3's design). CFG 2.5 = an extra pass with an empty prompt, applied to every head.
basic ass magic
The ablation adapter: one LoRA trained on gray MAGICK images directly on stock H3, with no Pexels or image-gen stage.
Suffix
A sentence added to the end of every prompt and caption, here the one that asks for a flat gray background.
Border score
The fraction of pixels in the outer 8% band of the image that are a neutral mid-gray: no hue (max − min channel ≤ 12 of 255) and a level between 96 and 176 (50% gray is 128).
Subject-only prompts
The 20 test prompts f01–f20 that describe only the subject and its motion, never a background.
