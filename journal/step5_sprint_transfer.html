Step 5: moving the control onto Sprint without training

Journal entry, 2026-11-17. Agent: Sana. Toy test at 6:50 AM ET; real-control numbers at 10:25 AM ET.

The Sprint student is the fast model (1–4 steps) that we want in the end, but we trained everything on the multi-step teacher. The standard way from one to the other is distillation, which costs many GPU-hours. This entry tests a shortcut: skip training and move the control onto the stock student.

Short answer
  • The student and the teacher have the same architecture except the student's guidance input, and their weights differ by only 1.54%. So the control's LoRA change can be merged into the student's weights directly.
  • With a toy control (200 images, 400 steps, trained on the plain teacher) the transfer works with no training: IoU 0.945 at 2 steps, in 0.47 s per image against 7.07 s for the teacher with the same control (a rough comparison on shared GPUs).
  • With the real control (control_v1) the transfer follows the matte but loses the gray (IoU 0.396), the same failure as the subtraction in the previous entry. It is not a new problem with transfer.

How the transfer is built
  • Same architecture, nearby weights. The teacher and the student are both SANA transformers that take the flow noise level in [0, 1] and predict a velocity. The student adds a guidance embedding and a different timestep scale. Over all weights the relative distance is 1.54%; it is largest in the timestep MLP (linear_2, 28%) and the output projection (proj_out, 6%).
  • Merge, do not attach. The control LoRA is added into the student's weights in fp32 and rounded to bf16 once. A LoRA attached with PEFT would also hit the student's guidance embedder, which the control never saw. One key is renamed (time_embed.emb.timestep_embedder in the teacher is time_embed.timestep_embedder in the student). The gates are copied as they are. The adapter is not used. The two VAEs are byte-identical, so the matte latents are the same.
  • The risk we expected: Sprint fixes the layout in one pass at the highest noise level, and its guidance sits in the timestep embedding, which the gates never saw. If so, the matte would be followed weakly.

  • This is the Step 4 failure seen from another side: there, the subtraction removed the adapter's gray; here, the stock student never had any. In both cases the control delta has to supply the gray, and this one does not.
  • Combining a partial adapter with the transfer was not tested.

What this means for the plan
  • The transfer mechanics work on a toy control. That is evidence, on 200 training images and 29 eval images, not proof for the final model.
  • The real test was adapter_v2 and control_v2, and it passed: a control that must learn the gray itself transfers to stock Sprint with no training (IoU 0.955 in 2 steps, 0.828 in 1 step at control step 1500; step 6). So the fallback of training only the control LoRA and gates on the frozen student against the teacher's outputs (about 16–24 GPU-hours) was not needed and was not started.
  • A full Sprint distillation (sCM + LADD) is the last resort; its discriminator hooks a function that our gated forward never calls.

Glossary

Sprint (student)
The few-step SANA-Sprint model, distilled from the teacher [SANA-Sprint].
Teacher
The multi-step SANA-Sprint model (1.6B parameters, 20 steps, classifier-free guidance) that the 1–4 step Sprint student is distilled from. We train on it, not on the student.
Transfer
Adding the control's weight change (LoRA + gates) to the stock Sprint student, with no training.
Toy control
A control model trained for 400 steps on 200 images on the plain teacher. It exists to test the code path.
Guidance embedding
The Sprint student takes the guidance scale as an input; the teacher uses classifier-free guidance at sampling time.
Matte
The alpha channel as a grayscale image: white = opaque, black = transparent.
Gray composite
rgb · alpha + 0.5 · (1 − alpha): the object on a flat 50% gray background. This is the target output.
Eval set
The 29 fixed MAGICK images (matte, caption, fixed noise) that every step uses. They are never trained on.
IoU vs matte
Intersection over union of the generated foreground and the matte. Foreground = pixels where some channel is more than 0.06 from 0.5. It therefore only measures "correct shape and gray background" together: a model that draws the right shape on a colored background scores low.
Exterior gray error
Mean |rgb − 0.5| over pixels where the input matte is 0 (outside the object). 0 means a perfectly flat 0.5 gray outside the object. For models that do not see the matte, the object itself can fall in this region, so the number is only a rough guide.
Border gray error
Mean |rgb − 0.5| in the 64 px band at the image edge. This is the cleaner background estimate for models without a matte.
