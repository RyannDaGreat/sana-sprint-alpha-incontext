H3 Magic: the split adapter (gray, white, black, alpha, RGBA)

Same 20 prompts and fixed seeds as the results page. Every item is the unmodified 512×512 output; each section shows its weight equation.

The split adapter adds two more outputs to H3: next to the subject on 50% gray, the same subject on white and on black. From the three, a per-pixel least-squares fit recovers the alpha (transparency) and the subject's true color, so the model outputs a cut-out (RGBA).

How it works: H3's last layer (24 latent channels × a 2×2 patch = 96 numbers per patch) gets three copies, gray, white and black. The copies start equal to the original (a zero-initialized difference Δ), and one new adapter (a LoRA plus that difference) learns to make them differ. All three share one denoising run: the model follows the gray image as before, and at the last step each copy gives its own clean image. γ scales the whole split adapter: γ = 0 is exactly the model without it.

It was trained twice: on top of the full adapter chain (Pexels + image-gen + gray magic, baked in; 269 steps), and on top of the "basic ass magic" ablation adapter (276 steps). Training data: the same 10,000 MAGICK images, composited on gray, white and black. The gray head keeps the guidance loss of the other adapters (so it works at CFG 1); the white and black heads learn the gray target plus their exact difference from gray. Every item is the 512×512 original.

Findings
  • It splits. On the subject-only prompts the white head draws the subject on pure white and the black head on black, and the least-squares fit gives clean cut-outs: dancer, kite, hummingbird, dog, cat. This holds on both the full chain and the "basic ass magic" ablation.
  • γ = 0 is exact. With the split adapter at 0 the gray output is byte-identical to the base row (table below).
  • The gray head is not hurt. Its validation loss stayed at or below its starting value (3.23 → 3.13 for the chain, 3.20 → 3.24 for the ablation, whose best was 3.20 at step 175).
  • What fails. (1) A soft halo, about one 32-pixel patch wide, around subjects. (2) White parts of white objects (a white cup or saucer) come out partly transparent: on white, a white object and the background look the same. (3) With scene prompts (main set) the background is a real scene, so there is nothing to cut out. Yet the split still makes bright areas (sky, windows) partly transparent: it has partly learned "bright = background".
  • Video: the split fades after the first frame. The split adapter was trained on single images only. In video, frame 0 splits as cleanly as an image. In later frames the white head's background drifts to light gray and the black head's to dark gray, so about 0.2–0.3 alpha is left in the background (it looks like a faint gray veil on the checkerboard). The subject's matte stays good and follows the motion. Gray surfaces that are part of the scene (a road under a car) get partly cut out too.
  • The split adapter alone on stock H3 (W_H3 + 0·Δ_pexels + 0·Δ_imagegen + 0·Δ_graymagic + 1·Δ_split, CFG 2.5 and CFG 1): without the gray magic adapter, H3 draws a full scene even for subject-only prompts (dog on a lawn, car on a track), so the three heads stay almost equal and the fit leaves the scene mostly opaque. Where H3 happens to draw a plain studio wall (the skater), the white head turns the wall white and the cut-out is partly right. So the split adapter does not bring the plain background by itself: it needs the gray magic adapter under it.
  • One training fix was needed. The first recipe (the guidance loss applied to every head) raised the gray head's loss from 3.23 to 4.50 in 50 steps. Giving white and black the gray head's guided target plus their plain difference from gray, and a 10× learning rate for the head's Δ, fixed it (gray 3.27 at step 25 and back to 3.23 at step 50).

Glossary
H3
MiniMax H3, a text-to-video model (480p class). We use it at 512 × 512, 141 frames (5.875 s) at 24 fps, 30 steps. It can also make a single frame, which is an image.
Adapter / LoRA
A small set of extra weights (rank 16) trained on top of a big model. It is a weight difference Δ that gets added to the model.
Bake
Add an adapter into the model weights for good (weight 1), then train the next adapter on top. Each adapter here is trained on a model with all earlier adapters baked in.
MAGICK
Our dataset of images of objects, with alpha mattes. Here we use its RGB composited on a 50% gray background, 512 × 512.
Gray magic
The adapter trained on 10,000 MAGICK images on 50% gray. The captions never mention the gray, so the adapter must learn it. The goal: a subject on a flat gray background.
Split adapter (Δ_split)
The adapter on this page: a LoRA plus a difference Δ on H3's last layer that makes three outputs (gray, white, black background) instead of one.
Head
One of the three copies of the last layer: the gray head (the model's usual output), the white head and the black head.
γ (gamma)
How much the split adapter is applied. 0 = off (the three heads are equal and the gray one is exactly the base model), 1 = fully on.
Alpha
Transparency per pixel. Shown as gray: white = opaque subject, black = clear background.
RGBA
The cut-out: color plus alpha. Shown over a checkerboard, so clear parts show the checkerboard.
Least squares fit
Per pixel, the alpha and color that best explain the three images C_k = α·F + (1 − α)·b_k for backgrounds b_k = 0.5, 1, 0 (iterative, box-constrained to [0, 1]).
Residual
How far the three heads are from one consistent cut-out (RMS, 0 to 1). 0 means they agree perfectly.
CFG
Classifier-free guidance scale. CFG 1 = one model pass per step (H3's design). CFG 2.5 = an extra pass with an empty prompt, applied to every head.
basic ass magic
The ablation adapter: one LoRA trained on gray MAGICK images directly on stock H3, with no Pexels or image-gen stage.
