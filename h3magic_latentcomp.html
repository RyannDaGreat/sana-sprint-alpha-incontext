H3 Magic: the Latent Compositor

Manifest §24 (m00983, m00984, m00985). Rounds scored so far: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (of up to 7: round 0 = baselines). Every image is the original 1024×1024 PNG, byte for byte.

The question: given the H3 video VAE's latents of an alpha matte (encoded as a gray image, white = opaque), a foreground and a background, can a small network output the latent of the composite α·fg + (1−α)·bg directly, without decoding the three latents, blending the pixels and encoding again? It has to be precise (it will be used inside training losses) and cheap to backpropagate through. Decode→blend→encode is the reference to beat on cost and the precision yardstick, never the method.

Why it is hard: one latent cell stands for a 16×16 pixel patch. A hair strand, a soft edge or a semi-transparent veil lives inside a cell, and the composite is a product (α·fg), not a sum, so a plain weighted average of latents is wrong exactly where it matters.

Findings

On TILED latents (what H3 expects; the primary score from round 5):

  • Track H, TILED latents: r5/agent_4 (agent_4): 29.46 dB (hard soft 28.87, LPIPS 0.0707, latent MSE 0.0267); 4,055,728 params, 48.31 ms forward + backward.
  • Track L, TILED latents: r5/agent_6 (latent-guided-filter-tiled-refit): 29.23 dB (hard soft 28.65, LPIPS 0.0723, latent MSE 0.0242); 1,464,080 params, 4.75 ms forward + backward.
  • Track L ≤ 250 k params, TILED latents: r5/agent_7 (tiny-tiled-refit): 29.07 dB (hard soft 28.48, LPIPS 0.0750, latent MSE 0.0294); 188,464 params, 2.21 ms forward + backward.
  • TILED references: oracle 30.10 dB; decode→blend→encode 27.05 dB (LPIPS 0.0866).

On the untiled latents of rounds 0–4:

  • Track L: r4/ensemble_light (ensemble-light): 29.02 dB on EVAL, 28.48 dB on the hard soft mattes, LPIPS 0.0790; 2,539,872 params, 5.51 ms forward + backward.
  • Track H: r4/ensemble_unet_swin (ensemble-unet-swin): 29.44 dB on EVAL, 28.99 dB on the hard soft mattes, LPIPS 0.0733; 7,491,552 params, 80.72 ms forward + backward.
  • References: decode→blend→encode 26.15 dB at 6640 ms per sample; oracle (the true composite latent, decoded) 29.57 dB: what a perfect compositor scores (PSNR can still be gamed by blurrier latents, see r0/taeh3_dbe; read LPIPS and latent MSE beside it).

What each round taught
  • Round 0. Baselines. Oracle (the true composite latent, decoded) 29.57 dB is the ceiling of latent fidelity. Decode→blend→encode: 26.15 dB at 6.6 s per sample (two VAE round trips lose ~3.4 dB). Closed-form ridge over 3×3 cells: 25.13; plain latent lerp: 23.47. The foreground over 50% gray is the easiest input (ridge latent MSE 0.036 vs 0.044 premultiplied, 0.050 straight). Diagnostics: edge cells hold 74% of the ridge's error, and giving the linear model the TRUE sub-cell alpha changes nothing (0.0362 → 0.0357): the limit is capacity, not information. TAEHV's tiny H3 VAE doing decode→blend→encode reaches 27.25 dB with a latent 10× further from the target: decoded PSNR rewards blur.
  • Round 1. Eight founders, each a cited idea. Every learned model beats decode→blend→encode. Best: a 1.27 M-parameter gated ResNet trained first on latent MSE, then 20 minutes with a loss through the frozen H3 decoder (pixel L1 + LPIPS on 256² crops vs the true composite): 28.36 dB at 4 ms forward+backward, +1.0 dB over the same net without that stage. DecFormer-lite (NAFNet U-Net, gate + residual output, 4 M params) 27.58 with the best latent fit; a 188 k-parameter per-cell MLP with depthwise mixing 27.37 at 2 ms; windowed transformer 27.36; virtual-pixel hourglass 27.31; residual on the ridge 26.79; bilinear hypernetwork 26.61; sub-cell matte head 25.99 (its matte decodes the alpha latent to 42 dB but drifted in joint training).
  • Round 2. Crossovers and new bets. Latent-MSE training saturates: the U-Net + Swin hybrid and the U-Net with frozen sub-cell matte features reach latent MSE 0.0099 but 27.5 dB; hard-matte training data + exact identity triplets + hard-example mining gives the best latent MSE (0.0086) and +0.26 dB on the hard soft mattes. Decoder-feature matching (LPL) +0.5 dB (27.85). The ridge prior + alpha-conditioned low-rank operator + SimpleGate/RepVGG blocks: 27.09 (+0.30 over its ridge-residual parent, 0.22 M params). No gain: knowledge distillation on recombined triplets, an invertible linearizing coupling network, a low-rank bilinear (compressed-domain) warm start, decoder-Jacobian-whitened MSE.
  • Round 3. The pixel stage on top of a strong latent model compounds. DecFormer-lite U-Net (r1) + the decoded-pixel stage: 29.34 dB (hard soft 28.89, LPIPS 0.0745, latent MSE 0.0116, 43 ms) — 0.23 dB from the oracle. The same with frozen sub-cell matte features: 29.25 (the matte features do NOT help: their own control beat them). Windowed transformer trained on hard mattes + exact identities, then pixel: 29.10 with the best latent MSE (0.0093) at 25 ms; U-Net + pixel + LPL 29.11. Track L: the 1.27 M ResNet with pixel + LPL + hard data + identities 28.90 (LPIPS 0.0787, 2.9 ms); training the pixel stage through the tiny TAEHV decoder as a proxy 28.71 (cheaper per step, not better); sparse edge refiner 28.55, but its base alone gets 28.53 (the refiner adds nothing); hourglass + pixel deep supervision 28.44; the 188 k tiny net + pixel 27.93 with WORSE LPIPS.
  • Round 4. Diminishing returns. A two-model ensemble (U-Net + transformer, both with the pixel stage) 29.44 dB (LPIPS 0.0733, 81 ms: still track H), +0.10 over its best member; flip test-time augmentation +0.0. Breeding the round-3 winners further did not beat them: matte-feature transformer 29.19, refinement cascade 29.19, matte U-Net with LPL + hard data 29.14. Track L: a small matte head on the 1.27 M net 28.99, latent deep guided filter 28.96, sparse refiner on the L leader 28.93, light ensemble 29.02 (5.5 ms); the 188 k tiny net with LPL + hard data 28.87 at 1.9 ms (its LPIPS regression fixed: 0.0987 → 0.0796). Then a finding from another experiment (manifest §25): H3 expects latents encoded with 256-px TILES. On a tiled re-encode of the same test set the tiled oracle is 30.10 dB, and the leaders, trained on untiled latents, drop to 28.92 (U-Net), 28.56 (transformer), 28.83 (light): round 5 retrains on tiled latents.
  • Round 5. TILED latents (primary score from here). Warm-started fine-tunes on the tiled re-encode of the train set (only ~2,000–3,000 samples were encoded when the children started): U-Net + pixel stage 29.47 dB tiled (was 28.92 before tiled training; LPIPS 0.0716, 43 ms); transformer 29.15 (best tiled latent MSE 0.0201); latent deep guided filter 29.23 (track L, 5 ms); 1.27 M ResNet 29.16 (3.2 ms); the 188 k tiny net 29.07 (2.3 ms). Coordinator ensembles: U-Net + transformer 29.59 (LPIPS 0.0695, 76 ms, track H), guided filter + ResNet 29.30 (8 ms, track L). Tiled references: oracle 30.10, decode→blend→encode 27.05. Dual-domain FiLM training (untiled + tiled with a tiling flag) gave nothing over tiled-only fine-tuning (29.15 vs 29.17 control).

Data

Train: 8,000 composites at 1024×1024: a MAGICK foreground (straight-alpha RGBA, half of them rescaled to 35–100% and moved) over a frame of an opaque Pexels video (originals of at least 1024 pixels on the short side, a random time in the first 8 s, resized to hold 1024², center crop, random flips). Composited in 8 bit. Six latents per sample (H3 VAE, one image = one latent frame, 24×64×64, the deterministic posterior mode): the matte as a gray image, the foreground three ways (straight, premultiplied on black, over 50% gray), the background, and the true composite.

Which foreground? Over 50% gray. In every fitted model it gives the lowest error (closed-form least squares, latent MSE 0.036 vs 0.044 premultiplied and 0.050 straight); it has no undefined pixels (MAGICK's straight RGB keeps the original photo under α = 0); and it is what the H3 alpha model of this project produces.

Test (300, never trained on): chosen to be hard (m00984). 1,000 candidate mattes from the whole MAGICK collection (the softest by soft-pixel fraction plus random ones, ranked by edge complexity) were shown on contact sheets to a batch of 8 Sonnet agents, which rated each 1–5 for "intricate, easy to composite wrong" and tagged it (hair, fur, smoke, glass, lace, motion blur, ...). hard soft = the 150 top-rated mattes on random backgrounds; hard contrast = the next 75 on the most contrasting of 8 background frames (brightness gap and background texture behind the soft edges); random = 75 random pages. The 96-sample EVAL set takes the hardest 48 + 24 + 24.

Fitness rule (written before round 1)
  • Primary score: mean decoded PSNR on EVAL vs the true composite (H3 decoder, bf16, 256-pixel tiles). Ties within 0.1 dB: lower LPIPS.
  • Hard rules: no H3 decoder or encoder inside the module (latents in, latent out); differentiable; trained on the train split only; at most 45 GPU-minutes of training.
  • Two tracks, one winner each: L (≤ 10 ms fwd+bwd, ≤ 5 M params) and H (≤ 100 ms, ≤ 50 M params).
  • Genetic frenzy: up to 7 rounds of up to 8 Sonnet children; at most 2 children per lineage per round, at least one wildcard per round; every candidate kept.
Research (sub-frenzy of 5 Sonnet agents)

The closest prior work is DecFormer, "Your Latent Mask is Wrong" (arXiv 2512.05198): a 7.7 M-parameter network predicts the latent of a composite from two latents and a mask, as z = (1−a)·zA + a·zB + s with a per-channel gate a and a free residual s. On the FLUX VAE (8× downsampling) it reports about 41 dB against 33 dB for plain latent blending [arXiv 2512.05198]. Our setting is harder in one way (16× cells, the matte only as a latent) and the round 0 baselines measure how far plain blending gets here.

Glossary
Latent
The VAE's compressed image: 24 numbers per 16×16 pixel cell, 24×64×64 for a 1024² image.
Oracle
The true composite encoded and decoded again: the best any method that outputs a latent can do (the VAE itself loses detail).
Decode→blend→encode (dbe)
Decode the three latents to pixels, composite there, encode the result: the slow reference.
lerp / lsq
Closed-form baselines: a weighted average of latents with an estimated per-cell alpha (lerp), and a least-squares linear map over 1×1 or 3×3 cells (lsq).
EVAL
The 96 test samples decoded for every candidate (the 48 hardest soft mattes, 24 hard-contrast, 24 random).
Track L / H
Cost budgets: light (≤ 10 ms forward + backward, ≤ 5 M parameters) and heavy (≤ 100 ms, ≤ 50 M).
Genetic frenzy
Rounds of parallel agents; each round breeds the best candidates of the last (combining and mutating their ideas) plus wildcards; every candidate is kept and scored the same way.
FLUX.2 MSE
Distance between the FLUX.2 VAE latents (FIBO2's image encoder) of the decoded output and of the true composite: a second, independent judge.
